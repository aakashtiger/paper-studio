The six evidence tests

  1. Paper is the white. The ground is the paper hex; no pure-white paint in the specimen.
  2. Flying white. Wide strokes carry 3–9 bristle gaps; thin stems stay clean.
  3. Pooled edge. Three wash passes darken where their edges disagree.
  4. Painting-in. The four progress panels show the same specimen at first stroke, structure, detail and last stroke; the phone loops show the timed reveal.
  5. Annotation leader. A Caveat note on a leader line, over a paper halo.
  6. No vector outline. The edge is a darker brush stroke, not a uniform line.

Sound

Felt piano, fingerpicked guitar or a soft kalimba; paper brush rustle, pencil scratch, a page turn, a quiet stamp. Silence at the mist. Voice optional. No audio files in the style; music and voice are cleared per film.

Five dos, three refusals

Do

  • Let paper be the white; never paint pure white.
  • Give wide strokes 3–9 bristle dashes; keep thin stems clean.
  • Hold one hero subject and one note per view; one accent recurs across the film.
  • Paint structure before detail, as the form enters.
  • Leave with a brush-edge wipe, a page turn, a mist or a hold.

Refuse

  • A vector flower with a watercolour texture laid on top.
  • A glossy greeting card with gold, foil and a drop shadow.
  • A hard cut into a finished painting, or a caption on the mist.

**Allocation.** The six minima are 4 + 5 + 4 + 4 + 5 + 4 = 26.000 s, so 4.000 s remain. Remaining capacity is 4 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 24 s, so the remainder is shared at 666.7 / 833.3 / 666.7 / 500 / 833.3 / 500 ms. In whole milliseconds that is 4667 / 5833 / 4667 / 4500 / 5833 / 4500 = 30.000 s. This is not an equal split: the two plates carry 1.833 s of growth each, the title and quote 0.667 s each, the mist and close 0.500 s each. Fit check: 26 ≤ 30 ≤ 50. Timeline: title 0.000–4.667, plate I 4.667–10.500, quote 10.500–15.167, mist 15.167–19.667, plate II 19.667–25.500, close 25.500–30.000.

**Plates.** 1. The stairwell wall at four o'clock: a dado wash across the sheet, the ivy trained round the stair window as the arch, the "V" and the stamp. 2. The third tread: riser first, then nosing, then the wear at the front edge; the leader runs from the wear to the note. 3. The stair window's lower rail in close, the ochre object-wash pooled at its base, the line in the left column and the note with its leader below. 4. Mist: no subject, no caption, paper and wash only. 5. The third baluster: belly, neck and the pale place where hands held it; same leader, same note. 6. The third tread's front edge, unfinished: the last wash stops short of the form and leaves one paper-coloured gap; stamp, "V", closing line, date, hold. One hero subject per view; the note sits on paper and never over the subject.

**Director choices.** Paint-in is 1.8 s (the slower of the two offered), one painted form per shot. Shot 1 paints its ivy arch in 1.8 s, leaving 2.867 s of push-in; shots 2 and 5 paint their plate in 1.8 s and hold 4.033 s; shot 3 paints its window object in 1.8 s and gives 2.867 s to the leader and note; shot 6 paints its unfinished edge in 1.8 s and holds 2.700 s. Palette is `evening-blue`: paper #efe6d4, ink #262a2e, wash #8fa3b0, petal #c9a27e, accent #c65438. `deep` is the wash at three passes, not a sixth tube colour; a frame carries at most five pigments (paper, ink, wash, petal, accent), and the mist frame is paper and wash only, no accent. Camera exactly as the recipes: push-in 1.0→1.04 at the title, 1.0→1.06 at the plates, static quote, pull-back 1.0→0.98 at the mist, static close; easings as written, no added moves, no shake. Transitions are the recipes' own: wipe out of the title, page_turn out of each plate, wipe out of the quote and the mist, hold at the close — no cut and no dissolve. The vermilion stamp appears only in shots 1 and 6; no second accent enters. Seed `1210` for all six shots. 9:16 recomposition: display 44, body 24, caption 24, label 14, monogram 18; margins 9 / 8 / 11 / 8 percent; title and note in the left column, arch and object on the right. No cast and no photograph: the family exists only in the brief's names field and the "V" monogram.

**Sound plan.** One original solo felt-piano cue, unhurried, no drums, no voice — `content.body` stays type and is never read aloud; the cue is cleared for this film and no third-party recording is used. Paper brush rustle on each paint-in (0.000, 4.667, 10.500, 19.667, 25.500); pencil scratch for the hand notes in shots 2, 3 and 5 and for the closing line; a soft page turn at 10.500 and 25.500, the two specimen exits. Music is out at the mist (15.167) and stays out through the close; one quiet stamp thump at 29.000, then the hold to 30.000. Silence is structural here, not a percentage.

## Invariants

- **Paper is the white:** Shot 1 (0.000–4.667) is the sheet and only the sheet: the wall is a `wash` band at 0.35 over paper #efe6d4, the window's light is unpainted paper, and the ivy uses wash, petal and deep-from-wash only. Shot 4 (15.167–19.667) proves the same rule negatively — the mist is `color: paper` at 0.8 and erases to the sheet. No frame carries a pure-white fill.
- **Flying white:** Shot 5 (19.667–25.500): the baluster's belly is a wide stroke, so it carries 5–7 bristle tracks that break into dashes along the turn, while the neck stays one clean stroke. Shot 2 (4.667–10.500) carries 3–4 tracks on the tread's nosing and keeps the riser edge clean. Bristle count stays inside 3–9; no thin stem is given noise.
- **Pooled edge:** Shot 6 (25.500–30.000): the close's `unfinished-wash` is three passes of the same polygon; where the passes disagree the rim darkens toward `deep` (wash ×3), and the last pass stops before the tread's front edge, leaving one paper-coloured gap. Shot 2's `shadow-wash` pools under the tread and shot 3's `object-wash` pools at the base of the window. No edge in the film is a uniform line.
- **Painting-in:** Shot 2 (4.667–10.500) runs 1.8 s from first stroke to last — riser, then nosing, then wear, structure before detail — then holds and pushes in at 1.0→1.06; a pan never reveals a finished painting. Shot 1 paints its ivy arch in 1.8 s, shot 3 its window object in 1.8 s, shot 5 its baluster in 1.8 s, shot 6 its unfinished tread edge in 1.8 s before the wash stops. The mist is the one wash the rule does not cover; see Questions, "Does the mist paint in?".
- **Annotation leader:** Shot 3 (10.500–15.167) is the clearest: the `leader` path runs from the window's lower rail to the note "the hour the house kept" in the left column, over a paper halo, and the object is untouched. Shots 2 and 5 carry the same leader to the same note on their plates. In all three the note sits on paper, never over the subject, and the leader is 1.5 px ink at 0.7 opacity.
- **No vector outline:** Shot 4 (15.167–19.667): the mist's boundary is paper on paper — a rising edge that breaks and a wash that fades into the sheet — with no cloud contour and no uniform line; the recipe's own failure mode is "a vector cloud". Everywhere else the contour is a second, narrower brush pass that changes width: the nosing in shot 2, the baluster in shot 5, the stopped edge in shot 6. The only path ops in the route are the three annotation leaders and the mist's rising edge; no recipe draws a contour around a form.

## Questions

- **One caption, four jobs.** Six shots from five page contracts forced one repeat; I repeated `specimen` as a second plate. `content.caption` is then the plate note in shots 2 and 5, the note beside the quote object in shot 3, and the closing line in shot 6. The pack permits a repeated caption as "a running note" but does not say whether the close may share its words with a plate note. Is a distinct closing line intended, and if so where does it live in S1a?
- **What does the mist erase?** `watercolor_mist` has no slots, no image op and no source reference: paper, a paper-coloured wash and a rising edge. The S1a note says to place it after a quote (I did), but the recipe cannot name the painting being washed back. Does the resolver feed the previous shot into the mist, or does a wipe leave it erasing a blank sheet?
- **Where are the 9:16 coordinates?** The recipe `composition` boxes are normalized 1:1 placements. The pack fixes the 9:16 type scale and says title left / arch right, but gives no 9:16 placement for the arch (0.6–0.94 × 0.16–0.64), the specimen note column (0.54–0.92) or the quote object (0.64–0.94). Do those boxes scale — stretching a 0.46 × 0.6 plate into 9:16 — or does a placement table exist that I cannot see? I kept the words short enough for a narrow left column, but I cannot verify the note and leader placement.
- **One prop asset, three named subjects.** `prop.painted-specimen` is the hero asset in specimen, quote and close, while the style wants one named hero per view and the guide says the treatment may name what is painted. I name a stair tread, a stair window and a baluster. Is the prop parametric per shot, and what parameters does the pack expose — or does a real film need three prop variants, which is a pack change I cannot make?
- **Is a two-appearance accent a recurrence?** Only `watercolor_title` and `watercolor_close` carry `prop.stamp-seal`; specimen, quote and mist have no accent element. The style says the accent recurs and the mist may carry none, but in a six-shot page the vermilion mark can only appear in shots 1 and 6. Is that enough, or should a plate or the quote carry it, which the recipes cannot do?
- **No attribution slot.** `content.body` is the only quote slot and `content.caption` is a hand note. If a client's remembered sentence needs a speaker, the only place is the caption, which then repeats in four frames. Is attribution a voice/film decision, or is it unrepresented in S1a?
- **Is `evening-blue` proven?** The card, the proof frames and the pack's examples are all `garden-day`. `evening-blue` exists in `tokens.color.palettes` with hexes but has no proof frame at any aspect. May a 9:16 page use it in S1b, or is it a director choice waiting on S2 pixels?
- **Does the mist paint in?** The 1.2 / 1.8 s rule applies to "any painted form", with the specimen as the reference. The mist is a paper-coloured wash, not a painted form, and has a 4 s minimum with a pull-back. Is it exempt from the paint-in choice, or does it also take 1.8 s — and from which edge?
- **One label set for six shots.** `content.date` and `content.venue` are the only label slots; title, both plates and the close all read them. My film is one afternoon, so the repeat is true. But if the close needed the sale date (three weeks later), or the title the house's name rather than the room, there is no second label slot. Is a single label set the intended S1a limit?
- **What does `sequence[].count` mean?** The example uses `count: 1`. The guide says a beat role may appear more than once and each occurrence gets its own allocation, but it does not say whether `count: 2` produces two shots with two allocations or one shot. I avoided the ambiguity with two beat keys (`first-plate`, `second-plate`) that both map to `detail`. Is that the intended way to repeat a page contract?
- **Loudness has no field.** The pack says delivery loudness belongs to the format, but the format schema carries id, name, beats, sequence, durations, aspects and primary aspect only. Where is loudness recorded for the lead?

# Questions — The House, Measured

Every point below is one the pack left open or answered twice while this six-shot page was being built. Each item names the file that leaves it open and the choice this film makes, so the lead can see what the resolver is being asked to accept.

1. **One caption, three views.** `content.caption` is read by the specimen (the hand note), the quote (the note beside the object) and the close (the closing line). Per-shot notes are S2, so two plates must carry the same note over two different painted subjects. The pack does not say whether that note may be subject-neutral. This film uses "left as it was", which stays true of the key, the stair and the closing page, and it becomes the close's refrain.

2. **A quote's attribution has no slot.** `APPLICATION.md` says an attribution "goes in the caption or the body". The caption is already the plate note and the close's refrain, so in practice the attribution must sit inside `content.body`, where it counts against the sixteen-word cap. This film spends three of its twelve words (counting the dash) on "— Nell Danforth". Is that the intended trade, or is there an attribution convention the pack does not state?

3. **The 9:16 `specimenNote` box.** It is 0.22 wide and 0.18 high, which is 86 × 125 px at the 390 px authoring width. At 24 px Caveat the two-line limit holds roughly 16–18 characters, far below the sixteen-word ceiling, and the pack gives no character count or fit test for this box. What is the tested limit, and is 24 px the right size for an 86 px-wide note? This film keeps the caption to four short words.

4. **Stroke widths: which frame?** `tokens.json` names `wide` 8 px and `standard` 4 px, and `APPLICATION.md` says the 6 px bristle threshold is "at the output frame". If the widths scale with the 390 px page instead, `standard` becomes about 11 px at a 1080-wide delivery and would cross the threshold, contradicting the pack's own "standard … does not [carry bristles]". This treatment reads both widths as output-frame pixels. Which is right?

5. **Which op carries the wide brush?** None of the five page recipes has a stroke-width op: the washes are `shape` ops and the only `path` ops are the leaders. The painted form is an asset (`prop.painted-specimen`), and `tokens.medium.strokeWidths` names widths that no recipe op references. Does the renderer give the form's ribbon the `wide` 8 px brush by default, or does the director set a width per form? This treatment assumes the former and names the stair's nosings, the rule's faces and the arch's jambs as the wide strokes.

6. **`names` at 9:16.** `APPLICATION.md` says `names` is "one or two short lines (about 18–24 characters per line at 9:16)", but the `names` box is 0.46 wide — 179 px — and 24 characters in 179 px allows 7.5 px per character, or 0.31 em at 24 px, narrower than Cormorant Garamond's normal mixed-case average. The upper half of the range does not fit one line. Which limit governs: the character guidance or the box? This film uses an 18-character subject line, "the Danforth house", which may still wrap to two lines.

7. **One date, two views.** `content.date` is read by both the title and the close, and `content.venue` only by the title and the specimen. A film whose title date is the sale date and whose close date is the day of the walk cannot carry both, and the measured place disappears after the second plate. Is one date and one place for the whole film intended, or are the close and the later plates missing a slot? This film carries one date, "3 OCTOBER 2026", on both plates.

8. **Repeating a page contract.** The guide says a beat role may appear more than once in `sequence`, and separately that `sequence[].count` repeats a shot. It does not say whether two entries with the same beat are both kept or collapsed, nor whether `count: 2` and two entries allocate identically. This format writes the plate once with `count: 2`. Which form does the resolver expect?

9. **Mist after a specimen.** A specimen exits with `page_turn`, so a mist placed after it would follow a sheet that has already turned. The guide offers "treat the turn as the page break before the erase" but does not say what the mist then erases, since the new sheet is blank. This film routes the mist after the quote to keep the one-erase chain. If a director wants a mist after a second plate, does the mist erase the outgoing painting through the turn, or is it skipped?

10. **When does `mist-full` land?** The mist recipe holds both the wavy rising edge (`mist`, z 1) and a full-paper op (`mist-full`, opacity 1, z 2), with no timing on either. The guide says the shot is "the erase in progress, not an already-erased hold", yet the full-paper op is opacity 1. This film plans `mist-full` to complete on the final frame, so every frame before it is an erase in progress. Is that the intended end state, or should the full pass complete earlier and leave a paper hold?

11. **Subtitle type.** `BRIEF.md` §8 says "Cormorant Garamond italic for titles and subtitles", while `STYLE.md` says the mapping is closed — "title uses display; subtitle, names and body use body" — and `recipes/watercolor_title.json` gives the subtitle op `typeRole: "body"`. The frozen style brief and the pack's own recipe disagree. This film follows the recipe and `STYLE.md`. Which is the pack's rule?

12. **One seal, two sizes.** The same seal is boxed at 0.1 × 0.056 on the title and 0.12 × 0.0675 on the close — 39 px and 47 px square at 390 px. The guide says the seal may move between the plates and is one recurring mark; it does not say whether it may scale. May one mark grow about 20 % between title and close, or should it keep a single size and only move? This film keeps the pack's two boxes.

**Designer:** blind subagent, second test. I have read `KPOP-IDENTITY.md` and
`kpop-identity.proposed.json` only. I have not read `blind-test.md`, the v2
critiques, the reference frames or the reference film.

**Brief to myself:** a 30 s Paper K-pop film, new story, new cast, a performance
first. No sunflower, banner, lamp, desk, network map, P(DOOM) meter or paperclip.
No office worker, no AI doom, no wedding, no fable with no stage.

## 1. The film: *ONE OF US*

**Logline.** A moon-moth idol opens her set. Her registration shadow peels off the
floor, copies her one beat late, and wants the light. They fight for the
spotlight, then share the bow. The last image is one shadow on the floor.

**Why it is a performance, not a fable.** The whole film is a number on a stage,
in front of an audience, with a star, a chorus, a song, an entrance and an exit.
Remove the rivalry and there is still a show. Nothing is explained by a narrator;
the story is told by formation, lighting, props and the lyric.

**Performance mode:** `stage-show` (the default). Song-led, 30 s, 16:9, 30 fps.
The track is a licensed 30 s edit at 92 BPM, one vocal, no added foley. This is
the one production dependency: music is parked in the project, so the film
cannot be rendered until that decision is unparked.

**Star:** VELA, a moon-moth idol. **Foil / co-lead:** ECHO, her ink cutout shadow,
who becomes a performer in her own right. **Chorus:** the ECHOES, three identical
ink cutouts, small, one acid eye each.

**Entrance.** Vela steps through a torn paper curtain; the tear is the first
paper event. Echo enters from the floor: a flat ink shape whose edge peels up,
then stands. **Exit.** Echo folds back into the floor; Vela holds the bow; the
last image is their two shadows merged into one, and the merged shadow flares its
wings once. **Signature move.** Vela's hair-flip into a cape-wing flare. Echo
copies it one beat late; that late copy is the "echo" and the film's running gag.

**The show test.** Cover the lyric. You still see a star, a chorus, two spotlights,
a curtain, an audience and a bow. Pass.

## 3. Motifs (4)

1. **Mirror shard** - a six-sided paper shard, gold one side, ink the other. Four
   uses: the glint in the curtain tear (shot 1); the missing shard in Vela's halo
   (shots 2, 8); Echo's face plate, cut from the same shard (shot 5); the shard
   put back in Echo's head at the bow (shot 10). This is the semantic object: the
   missing half is the story.
2. **Spotlight cone** - a flat paper cone, alpha .10-.18, two tones. Five uses:
   the empty pool (1), Vela's pool (2-3), the split cones (7), the single cone
   (9), the two cones at the bow (10). Formal device; it is the stage's grammar.
3. **Torn curtain** - vertical paper strips, 3-8 px tear. Three uses: the
   entrance tear (1-2), the act boundary before the dark (7-8), the bow (10). It
   is a set feature, never a side bar: each side strip is <=8% of width.
4. **Offset double** - the hard +6/+10 registration shadow used as a rhyme.
   Four uses: Vela's shadow is normal (2); it is too long and has a face (4); Echo
   casts no shadow (6); the two shadows merge (11). The film's whole subject is
   the registration shadow, so the motif is the medium itself.

No motif appears in more than 40% of shots. No sunflower, banner, lamp, desk,
network, meter or paperclip anywhere.

# Phase 2 - after viewing the reference

**What I looked at.** The six crops in `styles/paper-kpop/references/frames`
(`chorus-stage`, `desk-diorama`, `macro-prop`, `open-sunflower`, `portrait-paper-cast`,
`throne-orbit`) and 15 samples from `out/claude-pop.mp4`, including the first
frame (0.5 s), the first character shot (2.5 s), the middle (12, 45, 95 s) and
the finale (143 s) and desk pullback (152 s).

**What the reference actually is.** The film opens on a black field with a paper
sunflower and a circular "CLAUDE POP" title; at 2.5 s it cuts to a poster frame
of the idol with a petal halo, "I SEE" type and the researcher small at the

blackboard, "PLEASE DON'T" over a sunflower face, "LIES LIES LIES" on black, the
"FASTER FASTER FASTER" train and "OPTIMIZING" button, the pink "LET ME FREE"
room, "SAFE ENOUGH" with the paperclip and the safe, the desert "there you are",
"PAPERCLIPS" with the typewriter, "TRUST ME" with the alignment agreement,
"RLHF GOES ASKEW" with the clipboards, and a dark beam - and it ends on a
**stage finale** (red/cream sunburst, chorus, confetti, "CLAUDE POP", "THANK
YOU", P(DOOM) 100%) and a desk pullback. So the reference is a **presenter-show
with performance numbers and a stage finale**, not a continuous stage-show. That
matters for every answer below.

## 1. Would my film read as the same channel? **8/10**

Against the six crops and the 143 s finale: **8.5-9**. The paper medium, the
cream face with lashes and blush, the bradded arms, the petal/halo silhouette,
the chorus of smaller dolls, the warm field with a strong accent, the poster
type, the hard shadows and the stage-with-audience are all there. My moth
crescent and ink-cutout foil are a new cast family but the construction is the
same; my torn curtain and spotlight cones are a new stage device but the
performance frame is the same.

Against the 156 s film as a whole: **7**. The reference's middle is a diagram
show; my film has no explainer middle, no blackboard, no chart, no P(DOOM)
readout, no blue skirt on the star. If "same channel" means the film's average
rather than its finale, my film is a **short number from the same show**, not a
compressed version of the 156 s film.

The single score I would defend is **8/10**, with those two baselines named.

## 2. Is it a performance or a fable/diagram? **A performance. The field that made the difference is `performance.showTest`.**

Not `stageShareMin`: the JSON's 30 s rule is ">=1 stage shot", and a 30 s film
with ten diagram shots and one stage finale would pass it. The field that
actually forced the whole film to be a number is the **show test** - "remove the
story: is there still a performance?" - backed by
`forbidden: "paper-fable-with-no-performance"` and the default
`performance.modes: ["stage-show", ...]`. I wrote a stage, a star, a chorus, an
entrance, an exit and a signature move because the show test would have failed a
town/market/drought concept. The `performanceOpener` field also stopped me
opening on a diagram.

So: v1.2 **does** fix the blind test's failure, for this designer, on this
sample. The spec text changed the output. It is one sample, not a proof.

| **Side bars** | **Best-covered.** | `field.fullBleed`, `field.edgeBarMax: 0.08`, `noCreamPlateWithWings`, `forbidden: "side-bars"`. A vertical edge-column probe can measure it; the Uber 19% bars would fail. | No bars. The curtain side strips are <=8% and part of the set, so they sit at the tolerance. The rule should distinguish a set feature from a UI frame; an 8% flat column could still pass as a "curtain". |

**The pattern:** the three failures are **named** in the spec, but only the side
bars are close to a mechanical check. The office-worker lead and the blue-on-blue
figure are still a reviewer's job. The v1.2 performance block is the same: it
changed my design, but it is prose and a human test, not a gate that can refuse.

## 6. What is still missing

1. **A performance check.** `performance.showTest` is human. `stageShareMin` for
   a 30 s film is `>=1 stage shot`, which the reference's finale alone would
   satisfy. A 30 s film of ten diagrams plus one stage shot passes. The check
   should read the score and the frames for star, chorus, entrance, exit,
   formation change and signature move, and require a **stage share for every
   performance mode**, not only song films.
2. **A figure/field probe.** The character bbox, the 3x3 region grid and the
   local-contrast sample are proposed, not built. Without them, blue-on-blue,
   centroid x, height sd and local contrast cannot be gated. This is the same
   gap that let the Uber film pass 24 checks.
3. **A reference classification.** The reference is a **presenter-show
   explainer with a stage finale**. The spec's `defaultMode: "stage-show"` and
   `stageShareMin: 0.30` are a target; the reference itself opens on the banned
   sunflower and is mostly diagram. The spec should either reclassify the
   reference (presenter-show) and give that mode its own threshold, or declare
   the performance block a `target` and exempt the reference from it. Right now
   the acceptance test and the performance block disagree.
4. **A presenter-show threshold.** `modes` includes `presenter-show` for
   "narrated explainer, business model, story short" - the exact Uber failure.
   A host, a curtain and a stage are enough to be called a performance. The mode
   needs a **number**: a chorus, a formation change, a signature move, an
   entrance and an exit, not just a host at a desk.
5. **The 30 s arithmetic.** The long-film edit distribution (1-2 s 28-35%,
   3-5 s 40-48%, etc.), the p10/p90 bands, the transition shares (flash 0-8% vs
   designed 10-20%), the motif max 40% vs 2-5 uses, and the camera shares vs the
   4-6 distinct-move cap do not close at 10-13 shots. My own plan leaves a pull
   at 18% (band 8-15), a close share at 27% (band 40-55), 5-use motifs at 45%
   (cap 40%) and a lyric at 85 wpm (band 90-100). The spec should publish a
   **30 s band** for each, or mark the long-film distribution `R` and check only
   the 30 s min/median/max/cuts.
6. **A brad-scale correction.** The reference's brads are 10-18 px radius at
   1080; the spec says 3.0-4.6. A film built to the spec loses the visible joint
   that makes the cast paper-pop. Measure the reference's brads and reconcile
   `edge.bradRadiusPx`.
7. **A chorus palette rule.** `cast.chorus: "identical rig, different palette"`
   - my three Echoes are identical palette (ink/cream/acid), so they would fail
   the literal rule. The reference's chorus has different flower heads and
   torso colours. The spec should say whether "different palette" is per dancer
   or per film, and whether a shadow chorus is allowed to be uniform.
8. **A blue costume rule.** The reference's blue skirt/boots are a signature,
   but the blue-on-blue rule makes them unsafe in the one blue act. The spec
   needs a costume-blue allowance outside the blue act, or a local-contrast
   exception for a warm torso with a blue skirt.
9. **A diegetic readout and a type-density target.** The reference's P(DOOM)
   meter and its poster type are co-stars. The spec makes the HUD optional and
   gives only a hook count, so a compliant film can feel quieter than the
   channel. Suggest a new readout (shard count, spotlight meter) and a film-wide
   display share.
10. **A music dependency statement.** `voiceLedRequires: presenter-show` plus
    parked music means the only producible performance today is the presenter-
    show - the mode closest to the failed explainer. Either allow a voice-led
    stage-show (a spoken/chanted number) or state that a licensed track is a
    hard prerequisite for stage-show and creature-show.

## 7. What the spec caught in my own concept

The numbers were usable. They caught four errors in my Phase 1 plan:

| My number | Spec band | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Lyric: 38 words / 26.7 s = **85.4 wpm** | `voice.songWpm: 90-100` | **Fail** - add two words or end the vocal at 26.5 s (40 words / 25.3 s = 94.9) |
| Close share: 3/11 = **27%** (plus extreme 9%) | `composition.framingMix.close: 40-55%` | **Fail** - make shots 4 and 5 medium/close; close 5/11 = 45% |
| Pull: 2/11 = **18%** | `camera.pull 8-15` (MD) / `pushPull 30-45` (JSON) | **Fail** by 3 points on the MD band; combined 45.5 is 0.5 over the JSON band. Not fixable at 11 shots with 6 moves. |
| Motifs: 5 uses = **45%** of 11 shots | `motifs.maxShotShare: 0.40` | **Fail** - 4 uses = 36% is the most an 11-shot film can take |
| Chorus: identical palette | `cast.chorus: "different palette"` | **Fail** - give each Echo a different shard/eye colour |
| Iris: 1/10 cuts = 10%; hard 90% | `iris 8-15`, `hard 80-90`, `designed 10-20` | **Pass**, at the boundary. A single flash would be 10% and fail the 0-8% flash band; the spec's own worked example uses one flash on 10 shots, which would fail. |

That is the honest result: **v1.2 produces a performance**, but its 30 s
arithmetic is a set of long-film bands wearing a short-film hat. The design
instinct is right; the numbers need a short-format pass.

## 8. Verdict

**8/10, a performance, on-channel with the reference's finale and crops, not
with its explainer average.** The field that made the difference is
`performance.showTest` plus `pureFable: false`, not `stageShareMin`. The largest
remaining gaps are: the reference is a presenter-show but the spec defaults to
stage-show; the performance block is a target mislabelled as an invariant; the
office-worker and blue-on-blue failures are still human checks; the brad size is
mismeasured; and the 30 s edit/transition/motif/camera arithmetic does not
close. The film I would now make is **ONE OF US** with a crescent moonburst
field, a diegetic shard count, a blue costume accent outside the cobalt act, and
a four-use motif set - the same show, closer to the channel, still not the
reference's film.

# Blind test — a new Paper K-pop film from the spec alone

**Designer:** blind subagent. I have read `KPOP-IDENTITY.md` and `kpop-identity.proposed.json`. I have not opened the reference frames or `out/claude-pop.mp4`.
**Task:** design a 30 s film that is the same channel, with a new story and a new cast.
**Date:** 4 November 2026.

## Phase 1 — the concept, written before looking

### Title: **RAIN CHECK**

A drought fable. A paper town has had no rain for ninety days. A snail rain-seller, MISO, owns the last cloud, kept in a jam jar and sold by the spoonful. A goat broker, TALLY, buys the whole sky with one coin and swallows it. The storm lives inside his coat. Miso is left with one drop and gives it to a seed in the pavement. The seed grows an umbrella, and the umbrella is made of the broker's coin and the stolen storm. It rains on the town from the inside out.

**Tone:** sweet-sinister pop. The surface is warm and handmade; the joke is about who owns the weather. Nobody is hurt. The broker is left damp and quiet, not punished — the tender beat is Miso giving away the last drop instead of selling it.

### The two performers

Both are humanoid paper-pop performers to the six-point test: bradded two-bone limbs, 7–7.5 heads, 580–760 units, three-layer head, face plate, one ground shadow, 8 poses, 14 expressions. The species is an **ornament and a silhouette**, not an animal head.

### Motifs — 4, each with a job and a transformation

| Motif | Material | Uses | Arc |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The jar** | glass = cream paper + ink edge, 3.5 px torn rim | 3 | full cloud → traded → empty → becomes the pot for the seed. The semantic object with the arc. |
| **The coin** | paper disc, milled edge, gold face, oxblood back | 3 | flipped as an offer → swallowed with the sky → flattened into the umbrella's top cap. Money becomes shelter. |

No motif appears in more than 3 of the 9-shot cut (33%, under 40%). No sunflower, banner, lamp, desk, network map, meter or paperclip.

**Narration — 5 lines, 90 words, 188 wpm, first word 0.8 s, last word ~28.5 s:**

1. Ninety days without rain, and the whole town had started naming the dust on its windows.
2. Miso sold the last cloud by the spoonful, one drop at a time, and never once looked thirsty.
3. Then the broker came with a coin the size of a window and bought the whole sky from her.
4. He swallowed it. The storm moved into his coat. The whole town cheered for him.
5. Miso kept one drop for the smallest thing she could find, and it grew an umbrella.

Line 4 crosses the act break and the cut on "coat"; line 5 crosses the iris and carries into the umbrella. The title stamps after the last word and holds 1.6 s.

### Signature shot

**Shot 7, the umbrella grown from the seed.** It is one continuous pullback: the seed at 12% height cracks, a single paper rib unfolds, then eight; the broker's coin flattens into the top cap and the stolen rain threads knot into the ribs; the canopy opens to full frame and the camera keeps pulling until the two performers are 55% under it. The shot exists only in this film because it resolves the story's two objects — the coin and the storm — into the one thing neither could buy. It is the poster frame.

### Same channel (claimed from the spec alone)

2.5D layered paper, orthographic camera, hard down-right registration shadow, full-bleed field, warm earth with one rationed cobalt field, a performer cast with brads and ornament slots, poster type as a paper layer, one authored paper event per act (drop, swallow, umbrella), cut on the word, one tender beat, one semantic motif with a transformation arc.

### Different from any previous film

Not an office, a platform, a wedding or an AI doom. A drought fable with a snail and a goat; a field script of kraft → marigold → cobalt → cream; a story whose threat is ownership of the sky; a signature built from a coin and a storm; an opening on a drop and a closing on a pot, not a flower and a desk.

### Production note — the shot-count tension

Seven set-pieces at 30 s is a 4.3 s average and a 4.0 s median, outside the spec's 30 s band (9–12 shots, median 2.4–3.2). The 7 are directorial units. The production cut list splits #2 (the queue / the coin), #5 (the coat / the coin) and #6 (the rain column / the drop) into two shots each, giving **10 shots**: 1.2, 2.0, 2.2, 2.4, 2.6, 2.8, 3.2, 4.0, 4.8, 4.8 — 9 cuts, median 2.7, 18 cuts/min. The 7-shot table above is the design; the 10-shot list is the score. (The spec's "9–12 shots" and "18–24 cuts/min" conflict at 9 shots: 9 shots is 8 cuts, 16/min. Ten shots is the real minimum.) Motif uses are counted against the 10-shot cut.

## Phase 2 — after looking

**What I looked at:** the six crops in `styles/paper-kpop/references/frames`, 12 full frames sampled every 12 s from `out/claude-pop.mp4`, and individual frames at 0.3, 1.5, 3.0, 60.0, 100.0 and 155.5 s.

**The reference in one paragraph:** a sung paper musical. A black title card with a vertical light shaft, a paper sunflower unfolding petal by petal, and curved credits; a cream lab stage where a small researcher in a lab coat and glasses stands beside a giant idol with a petal halo; a red throne room; a black void with a smiley and "LIES LIES LIES"; a mint museum; a pink bedroom; a dark-blue space; a P(DOOM) stage with a five-doll chorus line, a thermometer, a starfield and monumental type; a split-screen "KILLSWITCH / PAPER BEACH"; a dark stone wall; and a close that pulls back to a green cutting mat on a desk, where the idol is a drawing on a lined sheet and the researcher is the maker, with a mono credit block. The film is a two-hander about AI risk, sung in character, with a chorus, a HUD, a lyric band, and a handmade frame story.

### 1. Would my film read as the same channel? **5/10**

Same **workshop**, different **show**. The paper medium, the warm/cobalt palette, the 2D similarity camera, the cut-on-word rhythm, the motif discipline and the sweet-sinister tone are recognisably the channel. But my film has no **pop stage**: no host/star pair, no chorus, no song, no mic/crown/ring/spotlight, no HUD, no lyric band, no desk frame, and its cast is 7.5-head humanoids rather than 4.5-head paper dolls. A viewer would call it "a Paper Studio film", not "Claude Pop". Scaled-up poster type, a rain-gauge HUD and two-hander staging could reach **6/10**; with the 7.5-head cast and the narrated voice-over unchanged, it stays **5**.

### 3. Generative versus descriptive

**Generative — the spec gave me a real choice:**

- `sets.families` + `fieldRoles` + `palette.script` → the drought town and kraft → marigold → cobalt → cream.
- `motifs.library` + `perFilm: 3–5` + `semanticObjectMin: 1` → jar, umbrella, coin, rain thread; the jar's full → empty → pot arc.
- `cast.ornament slot` (flower/halo/crown/hood/mask/hat/ears) → snail shell, goat horns. The slot is a real choice, though the silhouette is not.
- `camera.moves` + `camera.byBeat` + travel bands → push, track, orbit, crash, pull, tilt; six moves, no run >2.
- `edit` bands → the 10-shot cut, median 2.7, 18 cuts/min. The spec **caught** my 7-shot concept.
- `type.behaviours` → stamp, fold, eaten.
- `opening/closing` types → object birth, loop.
- `tone` (sweet-sinister, one tender beat, poison accent) → the broker swallow, the seed, the umbrella.
- `hud` (optional diegetic readout) → I chose none; the choice was real, and I under-used it.
- `variation` + `forbidden` → it kept me off the sunflower, banner, lamp, desk, network map, meter and paperclip, and off the reference's cast. This is the spec's strongest generative part.

**Descriptive — only made sense after seeing the reference:**

- `cast.headToBody: 7.0–7.5`, `cast.joints`, `cast.limbTaper`, `cast.hand` — a **library rig**, not the reference's 4.5-head doll.
- `cast.face` geometry — a **library face**, not the reference's drawn cartoon face.
- `medium.tornEdge/fibres/paperShadow` — the **pack's texture code**, not the reference's flat print.
- `composition.centroidX/negativeSpace/"one focal shape"` — a **bbox average**, not the reference's two-hander.
- `type.size16x9` — **measurements** of the reference's type, not its compositional role.
- `hud.box 246 × 96 at 1634/40` — a **measurement** of the P(DOOM) meter; "do not copy the label" is the generative part.
- `sound.song` values — **R measurements**, not a requirement; voice-led is a product compromise.

**Misleading — contradicted by the reference, not merely descriptive:**

- 7–7.5 heads: the idol and chorus are ~4.5; the researcher is ~5.
- "torn paper": the characters are clean-cut flat vector; the torn edge is the desk sheet.
- "one focal shape / subject centred": the reference's best frames are two-handers with the star off-centre and a graphic between.
- "no default card": the reference opens on a black title card with a paper object and curved credits.
- "blue is an accent": the stage floor, skirt and space scenes are structurally blue; the P(DOOM) stage is majority blue.
- The six-point performer test: the reference's own human principal (lab coat, glasses, no ornament) fails points 2 and 4, yet she is half the channel.

### 4. The spec's gaps — what I had to invent

1. **The performance frame.** No invariant says the film is a pop performance: stage, spotlight, chorus, host/straight man, audience, curtain, mic, crown, ring, sparkle. I invented a drought town with no stage. Biggest gap.
2. **The cast silhouette.** The spec gives a 7–7.5-head rig that contradicts the reference's 4.5-head doll. I invented a snail shell and a goat coat inside the ornament slot, which changes the silhouette. The spec needs a silhouette law (head-to-body, column torso, tube limbs, no visible hands, skirt/boots), or an explicit "the 7–7.5 rig is a different instance".
3. **The face style.** The vocabulary (10 eyes × 7 mouths) is specified; the **look** (lashed eyes, irises, red lips, blush, tiny nose, smooth plate) is not. I invented a geometric paper face that would read as a different channel.
4. **The sound's identity status.** The song is scoped R and voice-led is allowed; the reference is a sung musical. I chose narration and produced a film that passes but does not sound like the channel. The spec should say whether song-led is the channel or a reference instance.
5. **The type's role.** Sizes and behaviours are specified; "type is a co-star, often the largest shape in the frame" is not. I used small stamps and missed the poster scale.
6. **The relationship composition.** "One focal shape, subject centred" does not describe the star + straight man + device composition, the chorus line, or the designed split-screen. I designed single-subject shots.
7. **The handmade frame / title / credits.** The spec demotes the desk to an instance and forbids a default card; it does not describe the **title object + curved credits** or the **desk pullback + credit block**. I wrote no title sequence and no frame story.
8. **The HUD as a stakes device.** The spec makes the HUD optional; the reference's readout is a signature. I chose none. A rain gauge (`0.0 mm`, `1 DROP`, `100%`) would have been the obvious generative choice.
9. **The per-frame blue stage.** The film-wide blue band hides the reference's blue stages. I designed one cobalt act, more conservative than the reference.
10. **The designed split-screen/duet.** The side-bar ban does not distinguish a UI wing from a two-panel duet. I avoided both.

### 5. The three failures — does the spec prevent them?

| Failure | My concept | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Office-worker lead** | Miso is a snail rain-seller in a slicker; Tally is a goat broker in a storm coat. Neither is a corporate office worker; the story is a drought fable, not a platform business. | **Pass** on the surface. Caveat: the reference's own principal is a lab-coat researcher, and the spec's six-point test would fail her; the ban is really about a **bland corporate pair with no stage role**, not about a lab coat. My pair has stage roles but no **pop stage**, which is a different failure. |
| **Blue-on-blue** | Act 3 is cobalt; Miso is tomato/cream, Tally is oxblood/cream; local contrast ≥20. The only blue-on-blue risk is Miso's **cobalt wellies** in the cobalt act. | **Pass with a fix**: make the boots ink in Act 3, or separate them with cream rain threads. The spec's rule is figure-share, not boot colour; a blind designer can miss the detail. |
| **Side-bar film** | Full-bleed kraft/marigold/cobalt/cream; no bars, no cream plate with wings; the jar and cart are in the field. | **Pass.** Caveat: the reference's designed split-screen (`KILLSWITCH / PAPER BEACH`) is a **duet**, not a side bar; the spec's ban would forbid it. |

**The fourth failure the spec does not prevent: the paper fable.** My film passes the three named tests and still reads as a **paper storybook**, not a **paper pop musical**, because the spec encodes the **negative** (don't be the Uber film) and the **measurable** (paper, field, camera, edit) but not the **positive genre** (stage, star, host, chorus, song, HUD, poster type, frame story). The three named failures are the Uber film's failures; the spec is a good **vaccine against Uber** and an incomplete **description of Claude Pop**.

**Process note — the shot-count and cut-rate arithmetic.** The brief asked for 5–7 shots; the spec's 30 s band is 9–12 shots, median 2.4–3.2. I wrote 7 set-pieces and a 10-shot cut list (1.2, 2.0, 2.2, 2.4, 2.6, 2.8, 3.2, 4.0, 4.8, 4.8; median 2.7; 18 cuts/min). The edit band caught that the 7-shot version was a treatment, not a score. Separately, the spec's own 30 s numbers conflict: **9 shots is 8 cuts, 16 cuts/min**, below its 18–24 band; 10 shots is the real minimum. The spec should say "10–12 shots" or count the out-point.

**Final line:** the spec is **generative about the world, the cast instance, the motifs, the camera and the colour script**, and **descriptive — sometimes wrong — about the cast's proportion, the face, the surface, the composition and the sound**. It prevents the three named failures. It does not yet specify the **pop performance** that makes the channel a channel rather than a paper studio.

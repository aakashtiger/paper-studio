# The next film: what this style is for, and what to make next

**Status:** superseded, 2026-11-05. The next film is decided:
`docs/proposals/lakshmi-and-the-lights.md` — *Lakshmi and the Lights*, 60 s,
Paper Myth. This file stays as the style-fit analysis and the Uber v2
alternative.

## What Paper K-pop is best at

Paper K-pop is a **performance grammar**, not a slide grammar. Its strongest
materials are:

- one or two paper performers with a readable silhouette (petal halo, jacket,
  skirt, boots) and **a hand on the thing it acts on**;
- a full-bleed colour field with a floor, a torn lip and a practical light;
- scale rhythm: a wide stage, a medium two-shot, a close face, a macro prop;
- colour as a cut: cobalt, tomato, marigold, cream, ink;
- paper itself as the subject: a ticket, a stamp, a tear, a queue, a door.

That is a **music/performance film, a title sequence, a brand-world film, a
fashion/lookbook film, or an object-led short story**. A chorus can return
because music returns; the image must still change. A body can be in every shot
because the body is the subject.

## What it is worst at

A dense org-chart explainer. A ten-shot business explainer needs ten distinct
diagrams, each with a different device, and the performer is only useful when
the sentence gives them something to do. Forcing a body into every diagram is
what made the first Uber cut feel like floating stickers. The style can do it —
but only with `diagram-show`, a 20–60% character budget, one recipe per idea, and
a larger prop/diagram library. That is a **deliberate, more expensive** use of
the style, not its native form.

## Recommendation: *One Ticket*

**40 s, 16:9, 9 shots, 7 narration lines, 5/9 staged (56%, inside the
diagram-show band), 9 distinct devices.** No song pipeline, no music bed, no
org chart. One paper object passes through nine pairs of hands; the film is
about who gets in and who doesn't.

**Logline:** a ticket for the last show is printed at nine, sold at noon, bought
at six, refused at eight — and the fan is still outside when the house lights go
down.

**Why this is the right next film:** it is an explainer without a diagram, a
narrative without a song, and a performance film without a body in every shot.
The object is the protagonist; the body is a handler. It proves the flexibility
the user asked for at 56% staged and uses the style's own materials — paper,
tear, stamp, queue, turnstile, spotlight — as the subject.

Five staged shots, four pure-object shots, two characters and two handlers, no
recipe twice. The Fan leads three shots; the Clerk, Scalper and Usher are one
shot each. That is what “characters optional” looks like.

**Narration sketch** — seven lines, ~101 words; the measured voice decides the
cuts, not a 3 s grid:

1. “This ticket is printed at nine in the morning, for a show that starts at eight.”
2. “By noon it is folded in a scalper's coat, priced at three times its face.”
3. “At six, a fan pays for it with a phone, a card, and a promise.”
4. “At seven, the queue moves one person at a time, past a rope and a sign.”
5. “At eight, the turnstile reads the code, and the ticket tears in half.”
6. “The fan is still outside when the house lights go down.”
7. “Inside, the stage is full of people who are not her.”

The point: access is a system of small paper decisions; the object can fail the
person. It is wry, human, and native to a style that already lives in the music
and tour world. It is the correction to Uber: **follow the object, not the org
chart.**

**Why it suits Paper K-pop:**

- The ticket *is* paper. The tear, the stamp, the barcode and the stub are the
  material's native gestures.
- Scale rhythm is built in: macro (printer, stub), medium (coat, phone, queue),
  wide (seats, stage).
- Colour marks the journey: cream printer, red scalper, blue phone, acid
  turnstile, ink house.
- Every staged shot has a hand on the object. The “float” is fixed by contact,
  not by a `why` field.
- At 56% staged it proves the flexible mode: not a stage-show retreat, not a
  ten-diagram explainer.
- The venue grammar — ticket, box office, queue, turnstile, seat, stage — is
  reusable for the style's native music, tour and release work. The Uber v2
  recipes are not.

**Cost:** three or four new painters (`ticket_print`, `coat_lining`,
`turnstile`, `empty_seat`) and three adaptations (`window_dusk`, `corridor_walk`,
`macro_prop_beat`, `stage_wide`), plus a few props and maybe two poses.
Comparable to Uber v2's seven recipes, but the spend is reusable.

## The second choice: Uber v2

If the user wants the explainer fixed, this is the ten-shot plan under contract
2. It is a **compliance exercise, not the recommended film**. It uses one recipe
per shot, five staged bodies, seven new recipes, and cuts on the measured
clauses. It still has two diagram shots that are the same idea in different
costumes (four chutes, four meters) and a body budget that is honest but not
native.

| s08 | “so one network earns in more than one way” | `four_meters` | none |
| s09 | “More riders make each car busier, more drivers shorten the wait” | `busy_car` | Rider fills the last seat |
| s10 | “the network gets harder to leave” | `loop_corridor` | Rider pulls the handle |

The build is **seven** new recipes, not six: `unowned_shelf`, `match_ticket`,
`two_receipts`, `revenue_chutes`, `revenue_meters`, `busy_car`, `loop_corridor`.
The cut must move to the measured words (7.60, 14.56, 21.84), not hand-set 7.8,
14.1, 20.2. `unowned_shelf` needs a visual operator for “does not own” (empty
tags, a gap, a hand taking things away), or it is just a shelf.

## What must be true before frame one

1. **Temporal phrase binding** is built: `visual:check` resolves the phrase
   against `words.json` and refuses a phrase outside its shot. The next cut
   should move to those word times, not a 3 s grid.
2. **Contact staging** is declared: each performer block names the hand and the
   prop it touches; the probe already publishes `hand-left`/`hand-right`. A
   staged shot with no contact is refused.
3. **Type is blocked**: every display word goes through `TY`, no caption repeats
   the narration, and `audit:type` runs on the storyboard as well as the film.
4. **The recipe declares what it can draw.** 16 of 22 recipes have no
   `subjects`; backfill the ones the film uses with a still proof.

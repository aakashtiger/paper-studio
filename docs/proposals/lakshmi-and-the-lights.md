# *Lakshmi and the Lights* — a 60-second Paper Myth film

**Status:** decided, 2026-11-05. This is the next film, not another option.
**Style:** `paper-myth` 0.1.0 — a sibling of Paper K-pop, not a recolour.
**Format:** 60 s, 16:9, 30 fps, narration.
**Audience:** families and festival audiences; a shareable Diwali explainer that
is warm, clear and respectful.

## The decision

I am not making “Paper K-pop with Indian colours.” Paper K-pop is a performance
grammar: stage, chorus, pop-idol framing. Lakshmi needs a **myth grammar**:
iconic stillness, lotus and diya, cosmic scale, a narrator, and a source.

`paper-myth` keeps the paper-cut construction — torn edge, layered 2.5D,
halftone, one practical light — and changes the identity, palette, cast, rig and
motifs. The 0056 visual contract still applies: every shot quotes an exact
narration phrase, every device is unique, every body acts, no text on a face.

## The facts, and how they are framed

The film says “one beloved story” and “some say” where traditions differ. It
does not present one regional custom as the only Diwali.

| Claim in the film | Source / framing |
|---|---|
| Lakshmi is the goddess of wealth, good fortune, beauty and abundance; consort of Vishnu; four arms; lotus; red and gold. | Britannica, *Lakshmi*; BBC Religions, *Lakshmi*. |
| One popular origin story is the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the ocean of milk, narrated in the Vishnu Purana and Bhagavata Purana. Devas and asuras churned for amrita; Lakshmi emerged among the treasures and chose Vishnu. | Britannica, *Lakshmi* and *Samudra Manthana*; BBC Religions, *Lakshmi*. |
| Lakshmi Puja is held on the new-moon night of Diwali in most parts of India; some traditions call Diwali her birthday, others her wedding. | Britannica, *Lakshmi Puja* and *Diwali*. |
| Homes are cleaned, rangoli drawn, doors/windows opened, diyas lit to welcome her. | Britannica, *Diwali*; Google Arts & Culture, *The Story of Diwali*. |
| In Bengal the same night is Kali Puja; in the north Diwali marks Rama’s return to Ayodhya; in the west, many businesses open new account books. | Britannica, *Diwali*; Sahapedia, *Diwali: Origins and the Evolution of the Festival of Lights*. |
| Wealth is not just material: the stories place it with effort, virtue and generosity (dana). | BBC Religions, *Lakshmi*; Yale Hindu Life, *Maha Lakshmi, abundance and charity*. |

Sources are listed at the end. The film does **not** invent a quote, a family, a
city, a date, a take rate or a personal name. Human characters are roles:
**Householder**, **Child**, **Shopkeeper**. Gods are shown in their divine forms,
not as ordinary people.

## The script

Nine lines, 166 words. At 180 wpm that is ~55 s of speech; the remaining ~5 s is
breath and the final lamp. The measured voice will set the cuts.

1. “On the darkest night of Kartik, India lights a row of lamps. This is Diwali,
   the festival of lights.”
2. “Why Lakshmi? She is the goddess of prosperity and abundance. Her lotus rises
   from muddy water and still opens clean.”
3. “One beloved story begins in the churning of the cosmic ocean, when gods and
   asuras sought the nectar of immortality.”
4. “Many treasures rose. Then Lakshmi appeared on a lotus, radiant, lotuses in
   her hands.”
5. “She chose Vishnu. Some call Diwali her birthday; others, her wedding.”
6. “So homes are cleaned, rangoli drawn at the door, windows left open, diyas
   lit — a path for her.”
7. “Lakshmi Puja asks for prosperity. The old stories add a condition: wealth
   stays where there is effort, virtue and generosity.”
8. “In Bengal the same night is Kali’s; in the north, Rama’s return; in the
   west, new account books open.”
9. “Light a lamp. Make room. Share what you have. That is how the light comes
   in.”

## The 12 visual beats

One recipe per beat, one device per beat. The final cut may merge two beats if
the measured read is shorter; it will not repeat one.

| # | phrase | device | on screen | body / action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s01 | “On the darkest night of Kartik” | `first_diya` | an indigo night, a dark threshold, one hand lighting the first diya | Householder — strikes the match, lights the wick |
| s02 | “India lights a row of lamps” | `lamp_row` | a row of diyas runs across a paper street; windows open one by one | none — the lights are the actor |
| s03 | “Why Lakshmi?” | `lotus_darshan` | Lakshmi on a lotus, four arms, gold and vermilion, halo, two lotuses | Lakshmi — raises a lotus hand |
| s04 | “the churning of the cosmic ocean” | `churn_sea` | paper ocean, Mount Mandara, Vasuki as the rope, moon and stars | none — the sea and mountain move |
| s05 | “gods and asuras sought the nectar” | `churn_labor` | two rows of paper silhouettes pull the serpent; the mountain turns | none — the pull is the action |
| s06 | “Many treasures rose” | `ratna_rise` | the white horse, the wish-cow, the moon, the pot of nectar rise in sequence | none — objects surface |
| s07 | “Then Lakshmi appeared on a lotus” | `lotus_birth` | the ocean opens; a lotus lifts; Lakshmi stands, four arms, lotuses | Lakshmi — opens her upper hands |
| s08 | “She chose Vishnu” | `garland_choice` | blue Vishnu with conch and chakra; Lakshmi offers a garland | Lakshmi + Vishnu — the garland passes between their hands |
| s09 | “homes are cleaned, rangoli drawn” | `rangoli_door` | a courtyard, a broom, a rangoli of dots and petals, a child at the threshold | Householder — draws; Child — places a diya |
| s10 | “Lakshmi Puja asks for prosperity” | `puja_thali` | a low table: lamps, marigold, sweets, a small Lakshmi image; hands offer | Householder — offers; Lakshmi — blesses |
| s11 | “wealth stays where there is effort, virtue and generosity” | `ledger_dana` | an open ledger, coins, a shopkeeper’s hand giving a coin to a child | Shopkeeper + Child — the coin passes |
| s12 | “In Bengal… Kali’s; in the north, Rama’s return; in the west, new account books” | `three_windows` | three paper windows: a blue Kali silhouette, Ayodhya’s lamps, a ledger | none — three traditions, one night |

The final line (“Light a lamp…”) is delivered over the last two beats: s11’s
giving hand and s12’s three windows, then a close on the flame. No extra shot is
needed if the measured read lands there.

## Visual language

- **Palette:** indigo night, vermilion, turmeric, ivory, gold, leaf green; no
  K-pop neon, no glossy 3D, no photo faces.
- **Lakshmi:** four arms, lotus in the upper hands, red/gold sari, crown, halo,
  lotus seat. Stillness, not dance.
- **Vishnu:** blue, four arms, conch and chakra, yellow/gold; one shot only.
- **Humans:** paper-cut sari/kurta, bare feet, no named characters.
- **Festival of lights:** diyas, lanterns, rangoli, marigold, open windows,
  Lakshmi’s footprints. **No fireworks** — the film is about lamps, not smoke;
  it also avoids a pollution/safety glamour shot.
- **Text:** one small title, one source card at the end. No caption over a face.
  No big footer words repeating the narration.
- **Motion:** paper creep, a turning mountain, a rising lotus, a flame. No camera
  move that is the only movement.

## What I am not doing

- Not making Lakshmi a K-pop idol; not making a song-and-dance number.
- Not showing Kali as the villain of Lakshmi’s night; the regional line is
  respectful and brief.
- Not inventing a personal name, a quote, a city, a date or a family.
- Not using fireworks, glossy 3D, photo faces, or a caption over a face.
- Not repeating a visual because “the festival is colourful.” Every beat has one
  device.

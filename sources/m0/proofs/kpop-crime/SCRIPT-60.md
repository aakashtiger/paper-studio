## 6. What the 21 s proof has, what the 60 s adds

**The 21 s proof has:** 7 shots, 7 lines, 62 words, 21.0 s (~177 wpm); one shot per scene; days 1, 3, 6, 9 and 10; the cell once; the court once; the noose at the collar; a 3.0 s yard. It establishes the four men, the sets, the palette, the title cards and the spine: alley → bridge → cell → court → yard.

**The 60 s adds:** 21 shots, 14 lines, 186 words, 60.00 s (186.0 wpm); days 2, 4, 5, 7 and 8; the station, the train ticket, the hideout calendar pages 5–7, the checkpoint near-catch; the cell twice (s16 after the arrest, s19 after the verdict); the court's two-shot verdict (s17–s18); the yard's two-shot shadow execution (s20–s21). The calendar is now the day counter on every day beat; the gray hat is left at the checkpoint and found by the detective; the court jacket is brought to the cell and folded on the bench in the return; the knife becomes a drawing in the court file. The noose is no longer a physical loop at the collar: it is a shadow on the wall, and the bell and fade carry the execution.

**What stays:** the same four men, the same core sets and props, the same paper-cut look and terse voice, the same alley → bridge → cell → court → yard spine.
